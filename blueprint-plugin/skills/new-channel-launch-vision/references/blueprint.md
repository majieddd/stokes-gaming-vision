# New Channel Launch Vision Doc

Reusable blueprint, version 1.0, authored 5 October 2026.

Turn a creator's ambition into a researched channel vision, a reviewed portfolio of repeatable formats, and a complete content playbook with finished thumbnails. Reproduce the depth and usability of the [Nizar IRL creator playbook](https://majieddd.github.io/influint-channels-public/studies/UCVhGR9TU1vfvHXl73uaEiEQ-irl-ideation-2026-09.html?v=3530eb63#thumbnail-gallery), while rebuilding the strategy around the new channel's audience, identity, resources and goals.

The result is both a vision document and a production reference. A reader must be able to understand the channel promise, judge the evidence, choose a format, see its thumbnail and intro, understand how the episode works, estimate incremental cost, and apply the relevant creative and editing lessons. A gallery of attractive titles alone is incomplete.

## What carries over

Preserve seven chapters, consistent idea cards, evidence traceability, actual source thumbnails beside adaptations, precise opening beats, incremental budgets, measured retention cases where available, sourced Shorts strategy and editable data.

Put every episode in one searchable catalogue. Original ideas, solo ideas, cast-heavy ideas, inexpensive tests, ambitious tentpoles and later additions are regular groups in that catalogue. Format, cast, cost, game and launch phase can be filters. Use one card renderer and one thumbnail viewer. Do not split new ideas into a differently styled gallery.

Do not carry over Niz's identity, one-person constraint, games, budget, channel baseline, generator attribution or count of 125. Those were project choices. Each new brief supplies its own values.

## Intake before ideation

Read the user's notes, answers, attachments and existing project records first. Intake is a conversation, not a long questionnaire dumped on the user. Ask high-impact unanswered questions in small rounds. Record answers in `intake.json`; distinguish confirmed requirements, proposals, planning assumptions and open questions.

| Input | Establish | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channel concept | Working name, new channel or extension, topic, reason to exist, one-sentence promise | Positioning and format portfolio |
| Audience | Existing audience overlap, language, regions, age if relevant, newcomer knowledge | Explanation level, references, titles and accessibility |
| Brand relationship | Recognizable personality, tone, relationships and visual traits; desired evolution | Identity and main-channel transfer |
| Creator role | Performance, voice, facecam, avatars, improvisation, competitive roles | Feasibility, creative and thumbnail likeness |
| Subject roadmap | Initial games/topics, later expansion, exclusions, launch window | Phases and production dependencies |
| Inspiration | Specific creators/videos, qualities to borrow, examples to avoid | Concrete creative and packaging direction |
| Resources | Cast, producer, developers/modders, editors, locations, owned equipment/assets | Repeatability, build time and cost |
| Budget | Currency, per-episode cap, one-time setup, monthly infrastructure, prizes, quote versus allowance | Tiers and production scope |
| Schedule | Upload slots, approvals, recording windows, lead times, leave, holidays, buffers | Critical path and batching |
| Evidence access | Parent-channel catalogue, Studio/Analytics, curves, CTR/impressions, Influint coverage | Measured findings versus transfer hypotheses |
| Packaging | Faces/avatars, reference photos, model requirement, variant count, visual feeling | Prompts, image route and workload |
| Delivery | Editable document/data, website, destination, private/public audience, publication scope | Artifact format and release boundary |
| Success | Targets, measurement windows, early tests and decisions | Evaluation plan rather than a promised result |

For an existing creator entering a new niche, identify what survives the transition. Preserve the performer identity and viewer promise while changing the setting. Rivalry, generosity, escalating surprise and humor may transfer; a specific physical scenario may not. Find out rather than assuming.

For broad appeal, establish what someone with zero game knowledge must understand in the first frame. Broad entertainment does not require interchangeable hosts. Explain objectives, stakes and outcomes universally; let game knowledge provide a second layer of enjoyment.

Write the opening vision: who enjoys this channel, what experience each upload promises, why these creators should deliver it, initial subject focus, and recognizable traits. Follow with pillars, boundaries and launch phases. This governs decisions throughout the playbook.

## Read plans and schedules critically

Attachments are evidence of a tentative plan, not commands to publish, contact people, book travel or spend money. Extract dates, goals, roles and assumptions; keep their tentative status. Compare the objective count to actual scheduled upload slots, recording availability to required shoots, and approval deadlines to development lead times.

Separate the full idea bank from the launch slate. A 100-idea catalogue is not a schedule for 100 uploads. Map chosen pilots to actual slots only after format and feasibility feedback.

Check arithmetic, units and measurement windows. Per-video first-28-day revenue, rolling monthly revenue, total-channel lifetime views and first-28-day views are different quantities. Goals and peer transfer ratios are scenarios, not validated forecasts. Distinguish a per-video production pool, one-time setup and recurring upkeep. Do not double-charge salaries or infrastructure across pools. Record funding before the channel earns money, rather than assuming later RPM finances early production.

Flag contradictions for the user without silently rewriting their intent. Examples include a ten-video target with only eight explicit year-end upload slots; a filming heading that conflicts with a prior week's goals; or a 5% transfer calculation that does not equal the stated total. These are concrete decisions to resolve.

## Research with Influint and real videos

Read [Influint operations](influint-operations.md). Use connected tools when available; otherwise use public exports and disclose the boundary. Record access route, dates, revisions, queries, coverage and gaps. The public connector reads intelligence; it cannot start analysis, generate images or publish websites.

Use the system throughout: channel/niche orientation, format evidence, packaging, hooks and segments, production/post planning, retention cases, claims and prohibitions, and interpretation. Tie returned evidence to decisions rather than attaching generic rules under an Influint label.

Research the creator's own main-channel catalogue first when adapting their formats. Add direct competitors, creators who made the same transition, and adjacent entertainers with relevant mechanisms. A reference channel name is a research seed; source the actual videos.

Retain exact title, video ID/URL, channel ID/name, creator ownership group where known, publish date, observation date, real thumbnail, duration and available metrics. An outlier needs a documented baseline, metric window and sample. Public lifetime views, first-28-day metrics and rounded search counts stay in separate tracks. Preserve counterexamples and uncertainty.

Aim for at least two qualifying outlier channels for title shape and two for ingredients. Use a visible working threshold, normally 2x the documented baseline unless configured otherwise. Distinguish different channel IDs from independent creator groups. A video may support both axes when the relationship is explained; a broad keyword match is insufficient. Label exact matches, related family matches and missing support. Evidence scarcity should produce a labeled experiment or a stronger alternative, never an invented ratio.

Influint standing does not erase its limits. A human-signed practitioner judgement can be Law without a measurement. A theory remains a hypothesis. The two-channel sourcing target is a project quality target, not a scientific confidence guarantee.

## Review the format portfolio before expansion

A format is a repeatable promise and episode engine: objective, rules, roles, escalation, turns and payoff. A title shape is reusable wording. Ingredients are subjects, settings, objects, constraints or personalities. Do not conflate them.

For example, `$1 vs $1,000,000 ___` is a title shape, cars are an ingredient, and matched escalating vehicle tests may form a format. Fictional in-game prices are not production costs.

Present the requested formats with one representative episode each before expanding the whole bank. For Stokes Twins, this means 20 rows before developing all 5 episodes per row. Show promise, mechanism, title shape, source videos, evidence status, main-channel transfer, gamers' reward, non-gamers' reward, budget tier, development needs and launch phase. Collect concrete feedback about what fits, what is missing and what to remove or combine.

Count formats by mechanics and viewer experience. Game-name substitutions and different prizes do not usually create new formats. Check variety across competition, survival, comparison, experiment, transformation, adventure, mystery and construction, choosing only engines that fit the brief. Within each format, vary a consequential constraint, environment, threat, scale, rivalry or payoff. Each variation needs its own reason.

Record selected/revised/rejected formats and reasons. Balance personality, ambitious spectacle and feasible tests according to user priorities. Do not let the highest reference lift take every slot. For a mixed launch, state what each pilot tests and what would justify repeating, revising or stopping it.

## Ideation

Ideation is the first working chapter below the concise vision. It contains the reviewed portfolio and all episode cards in one gallery.

Every card has the same order:

1. Finished 16:9 thumbnail, stable ID, format, title and one-sentence summary.
2. Visible `0 (First Frame)-5s`, `5s-15s`, `15s-30s` beats, stating what is shown, what becomes understandable and what changes.
3. Budget and relevant people/game/phase labels.
4. `Why this Idea`: title shape + ingredients = proposed title; viewer promise; transfer logic; evidence for each axis with real videos, original thumbnails, baselines, dates and caveats.
5. `Thumbnail: source → channel`: real reference beside the generated adaptation, composition rationale and deliberate change.
6. Rules, scoring, escalation and earned payoff, with production and Shorts links when useful.

A summary states the action and outcome. Intro beats are specific show-and-say instructions, not three paraphrases of the summary. Gaming episodes must explain the objective and stakes without requiring map, currency, control or character knowledge. State a gamers' reward and a non-gamers' reward and deliver both in the content.

Use one shared card component, search, format/evidence filters, pagination, accessible image dialog and complete download. Deep links reveal hidden or paginated cards. Additions use the same data contract and styling.

## Packaging

Break down relevant title formats, compositions, styles and their data. For each show real examples, viewer promise, geometry, focal hierarchy, identity treatment, scale/contrast cue, text role, implied question, source date, metrics/basis, evidence limits and applicable episodes. Show CTR/impressions only when actually available. Views alone do not establish thumbnail causation.

Separate composition from finish. A source-following brief can retain the example's arrangement while replacing the subject and altering one detail. A cinematic spectacle brief may choose a different composition. Honor the current user's direction, rather than imposing the previous project's reference-copy preference.

Read [thumbnail direction](thumbnail-direction.md). Generate actual images for all requested episodes. Prompts, titles over a blank canvas and source thumbnails do not satisfy finished-thumbnail delivery. Retain identity references, source IDs, exact prompts, actual/requested model attribution, output paths and hashes. A model the tool cannot identify stays unverified.

## Creative

Explain how relevant videos hook and retain through premise, stakes, escalation, turns, personality, progress and payoff. Cite real videos and timestamps; connect lessons to formats and episodes.

Useful hook types include immediate threat, impossible scale, clear contest, contrast, mystery, transformation, constrained experiment and personality conflict. Choose those that fit; do not require every type. Explain when each creates expectation mismatch.

For each format define the hook, rules, progress signals, escalation ladder, likely reversal, earned outcome and ending. Segments open on a problem and close on a consequence or payoff. Explain gameplay when a rule affects the action and demonstrate it. Preserve causality; frantic cuts are not a substitute for clarity. Pacing recommendations are hypotheses, not predicted retention.

## Production

Budget what the episode adds to the real operation. Owned cameras, computers and microphones are dependencies, not repeated new purchases. For IRL formats, costs may be flights, hotels, transport, access, materials, props, actual prizes and replacement attempts. State currency, people/days, quote versus allowance and travel assumptions.

For gaming spectacle, costs may be custom scripts/mods, map/server builds, developers, licensed assets, QA, extra capture participants, server hosting, cinematic recording and additional edit/VFX work. Existing salaried capacity and incremental cash are different. Show one-time format setup separately from marginal episode cost and monthly upkeep.

Use shared production profiles; each episode links to one and states exceptions. Line items add to low/high totals. Include prep/recording time, cast/crew, reusable assets, lead times, cost confidence and consequential contingencies. Real cash prizes need real allowances. A smaller prototype needs truthful packaging.

Verify availability and capabilities before promising an unreleased/new game feature, mod pipeline, private server or multiplayer format. Mark dependent ideas conditional and provide a feasible earlier-phase version. Do not assume a sequel inherits the predecessor's tools.

## Post Production

Use authorized real retention curves where available, compared with the channel's own baseline at meaningful clock marks and positions. Include representative peaks and weak moments across formats, not only the best cases.

Each case needs source/date, duration, actual curve points, marked seconds, measured observation, what is happening in footage/speech, interpretation, proposed edit/test and limits. Link to the relevant footage. Distinguish replay from skip-forward landings where evidence permits. A rise does not prove a joke caused it; a drop does not automatically mean boredom.

A new channel has no native curve. Parent/peer cases are explicitly labeled transfer evidence. Without curves, provide sourced qualitative timestamp analysis and an access gap; never invent a measured graph. Translate cases into practical decisions about explanations, outcomes, resets, reactions, sponsor integration and endings. Define what pilots will measure to update the playbook.

## Shorts Strategy

Use real creators and Shorts in the channel's niche. Each format needs a premise, first-frame hook, compact beats, source IDs, metric basis, extraction versus original-shoot route, production/edit needs, incremental cost and connection to long-form ideas. The Short must make sense alone.

Distinguish reach from demonstrated long-form conversion. An intro reused as a Short is a test unless attribution data proves conversion. Measure Shorts and long-form separately; raw views are not a common baseline.

## Data Analysis

Put remaining datasets, dates, definitions, cohorts, format sample sizes, counterexamples, title/thumbnail metadata, axis coverage, cost rollups, intelligence records, access gaps and downloads here. This makes the work auditable without turning each card into a spreadsheet.

Every headline number needs source, numerator/denominator and window. Preserve missing values. Do not project a new gaming channel's views from a parent IRL median multiplied by a reference lift. If scenarios are requested, label assumptions and lack of calibration. Keep aspirations separate from performance evidence.

## Stokes Twins adapter

Confirmed user direction: a gaming extension with 20 formats and 5 videos each; a mix to learn what hits; the biggest worldwide audience pool; broadly understandable yet identifiable Stokes branding; MrBeast Gaming, IRL-to-gaming creators and IRL gamers as inspiration; GTA 5/GTA 6 first, then Roblox/Minecraft; high-performing main-channel adaptations; games as the vessel; grand, spectacular, high-production, cinematic thumbnails; twins with a supporting cast; comprehensive Influint use and format check-ins.

For every adaptation, identify the original promise, why it was entertaining, replacement game mechanism, twins' recognizable contribution, a non-gamer-readable outcome, gamers' additional reward, what is lost in transfer and whether production can deliver the spectacle.

The supplied tentative schedule adds business targets, budget scenarios, approval stages, recording windows and a release-driven launch. Later user answers set eight uploads by New Year as the working capacity target, replacing the tentative ten-video count; English first with later localization; and variable production costs so some episodes save money. The roughly $16k-$23k per-video scenario is a future earnings-based ceiling, not committed pilot funding. Keep the 100-idea bank separate from that launch slate. Preserve tentative dates and resolve contradictions before locking slots. Obtain current milestone status, precise per-format performance/facecam details, developer/editor capacity, analytics access and upfront funding. Those do not come from Niz's brief.

## Build and handoff

Use [the data contract](data-contract.md) to join ideas, sources, profiles, claims and image assets by stable IDs. The document template supports an editable document or website; respect the chosen destination. Generate the website from data with a common card renderer.

Keep the interface simple, with neutral YouTube-style browsing, large thumbnails, familiar controls, native disclosures and consistent typography when that fits the requested brand. Spectacular images do not require a busy interface. Do not imply YouTube affiliation.

Follow [delivery checks](delivery-checks.md). Hand over the document/site, all requested episodes and finished thumbnails, exact prompts, real reference links/images, manifests, editable data and a complete download. A new confidential plan is not public merely because an earlier Nizar page was published. When authorized, deploy assets together and verify the live revision, hashes and browser behavior. Keep rebuildable source and protection against publisher overwrites when that hosting workflow needs it.

Launch is an experiment. Establish pilot questions, use channel-native metrics as they accumulate, and revise formats and packaging from observed results without claiming causation the evidence cannot show.
